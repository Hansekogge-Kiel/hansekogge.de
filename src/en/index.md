## At a glance

The **Kieler Hansekogge** is a replica of the Bremen Cog of 1380. Her berth is at the Satorikai, right behind the Maritime Museum in Kiel (Germany). She is run entirely by volunteers of the *Förderverein Historische Hansekogge Kiel e. V.*

## History

- **1380** – An almost completed cog is swept away from a shipyard in Bremen by a storm tide, capsizes and sinks in the mud of the River Weser.
- **1962** – The almost completely preserved hull is found; the pieces are put together at the German Maritime Museum in Bremerhaven.
- **1987–1991** – A replica is built at the Rathje yard in Kiel-Friedrichsort within a job-creation scheme for young unemployed people. Keel laid on 12 June 1987, launched on 30 October 1989, put into service on 17 May 1991.

The disaster of 1380 is today's luck: before the find there was no detailed knowledge of these ships. For centuries cogs were the freight carriers of the Hanse, the great North-European trading union operating in the Baltic and North Sea, up to Norway and into the Channel.

## Typical features of a cog

- bottom carvel-planked, the rest of the hull clinker-planked
- strong cross-beams whose ends protrude through the sides of the hull
- planks joined by nails which are clenched back into the timber
- one mast with a single square sail
- a stern rudder hung on the sternpost

## Sailing with us

The cog sails the North Sea and Baltic, from Brest in the west to Riga in the north. She can be chartered for day trips and receptions at the pier (up to 50 guests aboard, up to 80 at the pier). Single bookings are offered at harbour festivals such as Kiel Week, and after-work sailing trips are held through the summer. All income goes towards the upkeep of the ship.
